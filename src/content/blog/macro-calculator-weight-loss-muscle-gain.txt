## What Are Macros?

Macronutrients — or "macros" — are the three main nutrients that make up all the food you eat:

Protein (4 calories per gram): Builds and repairs muscle, keeps you full, and boosts your metabolism. Found in meat, fish, eggs, dairy, and legumes.

Carbohydrates (4 calories per gram): Your body's primary energy source. Found in grains, fruits, vegetables, and beans.

Fat (9 calories per gram): Supports hormones, brain function, and vitamin absorption. Found in oils, nuts, avocado, and fatty fish.

Every food you eat is made up of some combination of these three macronutrients. When you count macros, you're tracking how many grams of each you eat per day.

## How to Calculate Your Macros

The right macro split depends on your personal goal. Here's how to set yours:

### Step 1: Find Your Daily Calorie Goal

Use our free calorie calculator at FitJourney USA to calculate your TDEE and set a calorie target based on your goal (deficit for fat loss, surplus for muscle gain).

### Step 2: Choose Your Macro Split

These are the most common splits used by US nutritionists and fitness coaches:

- Weight loss: Protein 35% / Carbs 35% / Fat 30%
- Muscle gain: Protein 30% / Carbs 45% / Fat 25%
- Maintenance: Protein 25% / Carbs 45% / Fat 30%
- Keto: Protein 25% / Carbs 5% / Fat 70%

### Step 3: Convert Percentages to Grams

Multiply your daily calorie goal by each percentage, then divide by the calories per gram:

- Protein: (Calories × 0.35) ÷ 4
- Carbs: (Calories × 0.35) ÷ 4
- Fat: (Calories × 0.30) ÷ 9

Example for a 1,800-calorie weight loss goal:

- Protein: (1,800 × 0.35) ÷ 4 = 158g
- Carbs: (1,800 × 0.35) ÷ 4 = 158g
- Fat: (1,800 × 0.30) ÷ 9 = 60g

## Why Protein Is the Most Important Macro

No matter your goal, protein is the macro you should prioritize above all others. Here's why:

It keeps you full longer than carbs or fat, making it easier to stay in a calorie deficit. It preserves muscle mass when you're losing weight, so you lose fat — not muscle. It has the highest thermic effect, meaning your body burns more calories just by digesting it.

Most fitness experts recommend 0.7–1 gram of protein per pound of body weight for active Americans. If you weigh 160 lbs, aim for 112–160g of protein per day.

## Best Foods for Each Macro

High-protein foods: Chicken breast, turkey, tuna, salmon, Greek yogurt, eggs, cottage cheese, tofu, lentils

Healthy carbs: Oats, brown rice, sweet potato, quinoa, whole wheat bread, fruits, vegetables

Healthy fats: Avocado, olive oil, almonds, walnuts, chia seeds, salmon, peanut butter

## How to Track Your Macros

The easiest way to track macros is with a food tracking app. Popular options in the US include MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, and Lose It. Simply log everything you eat and the app will show you your daily macro totals.

Tips for accurate tracking: weigh your food with a kitchen scale, log meals before eating, and plan your meals the night before.

## Is Counting Macros Right for You?

Counting macros works best for people who want precise control over their results. It's ideal for gym-goers, athletes, and anyone who has hit a plateau with simple calorie counting. It does require more effort than just counting calories, but many people find it more satisfying because they can eat a wide variety of foods as long as they hit their numbers.
