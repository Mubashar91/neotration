One rotisserie chicken can become five meals if you portion usable meat before planning the menu. This guide uses five 3 oz cooked chicken portions, or 15 oz meat total, with beans, grains, and vegetables to complete each meal. Bird sizes vary; weigh the meat instead of assuming every chicken gives the same yield.

Separate meat from bones promptly. Choose skin-on or skinless portions and calculate nutrition accordingly. Store prices differ, so this guide assumes neither a fixed Costco price nor one calorie total for every chicken.

## Portion before you start

Set aside five containers with 3 oz cooked chicken each. Refrigerate the first few portions and freeze later ones promptly. Save extra meat for another meal, or add another protein if your bird yields less than 15 oz.

Plain cooked skinless chicken contributes roughly 25g protein per 3 oz portion, varying by cut and preparation. Beans and grains add more. Skin, sauce, and oil affect calories; use package nutrition and actual portions for tracking.

## Five meals from the chicken

1. **Lemon rice bowl:** Warm 3 oz chicken, 1/2 cup cooked brown rice, and 1 cup broccoli with a splash of water to 165°F. Finish with 1 tsp olive oil and 1 tbsp lemon juice.
2. **White bean soup:** Simmer 1/2 cup white beans, 1/2 cup canned tomatoes, 1/2 cup carrots, and 1 cup low-sodium broth until carrots soften. Add 3 oz chicken and heat to 165°F.
3. **Yogurt chicken wrap:** Mix 3 oz cold chicken with 2 tbsp plain Greek yogurt, 1 tsp mustard, and 1/4 cup celery. Fill one whole-grain wrap with lettuce. Keep refrigerated until eating.
4. **Taco potato:** Cook one small potato until tender. Top with 3 oz chicken, 1/4 cup black beans, 2 tbsp salsa, and 2 tbsp yogurt. Heat chicken and beans to 165°F before assembling.
5. **Vegetable skillet:** Cook 1 1/2 cups mixed vegetables in 1 tsp oil. Add 3 oz chicken, 1/2 cup cooked rice, and 1 tbsp reduced-sodium soy sauce or lemon juice. Heat leftovers to 165°F.

## Shopping list for one person

- One rotisserie chicken yielding at least 15 oz cooked meat.
- Rice for three 1/2-cup cooked portions, one potato, and one wrap.
- One can white beans and one can black beans; freeze unused drained portions if needed.
- Broccoli, carrots, celery, lettuce, mixed vegetables, and lemon.
- Plain Greek yogurt, tomatoes, low-sodium broth, salsa, mustard, and olive oil.

Frozen vegetables are convenient when you need only small portions. Use remaining yogurt at breakfast and spare beans in a [dense bean salad](/blog/dense-bean-salad-meal-prep).

## Keep the fifth meal safe

Count storage time from when chicken was cooked or purchased hot, not from when each meal was assembled. Use refrigerated cooked chicken within 3–4 days. Freeze later portions on prep day and thaw in the refrigerator.

Keep the refrigerator at 40°F or below. Put leftovers in shallow containers promptly; do not leave the bird on the counter while cooking five meals. Reheating does not reset the storage clock.

## Calculate cost per meal

Divide the chicken price by the portions actually obtained, then add vegetables, grains, sauces, and other ingredients. A cheap bird is not economical if most goes unused. Our [budget meal prep worksheet](/blog/budget-high-protein-meal-prep) explains the calculation; [chicken potato bowls](/blog/chicken-potato-meal-prep-bowls) offer flavor variations.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use leftover roast chicken?** Yes, if cooked, cooled, and stored safely. Count the time already spent refrigerated.

**Can I eat it cold?** Yes, when fully cooked and safely chilled. Cold wraps still require refrigeration in transit.
