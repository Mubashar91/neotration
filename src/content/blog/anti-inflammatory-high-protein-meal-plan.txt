This seven-day meal plan uses vegetables, fruit, beans, whole grains, fish, tofu, and olive oil, with a protein food at each meal. It is a practical Mediterranean-style menu for readers searching for an anti-inflammatory meal plan. It does not diagnose inflammation or promise to prevent or treat a disease.

The menu uses no deliberately added sugar when you choose plain yogurt, unsweetened drinks, and sauces without added sugar. Fruit and dairy still contain naturally occurring sugars. Read labels: a bottled dressing or flavored yogurt can change that claim.

## Start with portions you can adapt

For breakfast, use plain Greek yogurt with oats and berries, or eggs with whole-grain toast. Lunch combines 1/2–3/4 cup cooked beans or lentils, vegetables, and a whole grain; add tofu or chicken as wanted. Dinner uses roughly 4 oz cooked fish, 3–4 oz cooked chicken, or 6 oz tofu with grains and vegetables.

These are examples, not personalized calorie or protein targets. Choose larger portions, snacks, or additional meals to meet your needs. No single spice or ingredient makes the whole diet anti-inflammatory.

## Three cooking methods to repeat

**Fish plate:** Put a 5 oz raw salmon fillet and 1 cup vegetables on a tray with 1 tsp olive oil and lemon. Roast at 400°F, allowing about 12–18 minutes depending on thickness, until fish reaches 145°F. Add 1/2 cup cooked grain or a potato.

**Chicken bean skillet:** Cook 5 oz raw diced chicken with 1 tsp oil; add 1/2 cup white beans, 1/2 cup canned tomatoes, and 1 cup spinach. Simmer until chicken reaches 165°F. Serve with rice as needed.

**Tofu chickpea skillet:** Heat 6 oz cubed firm tofu, 1/2 cup chickpeas, 1 cup chopped vegetables, 1/2 cup tomatoes, and 1 tsp olive oil until hot and vegetables soften. Season with cumin or paprika. This makes one generous meal or two smaller portions.

## Shopping list

- Protein: plain Greek yogurt, eggs, chicken, salmon, tofu, and one can light tuna.
- Legumes: cooked lentils, chickpeas, and white beans; choose canned versions if convenient.
- Grains: oats, whole-grain bread, brown rice, and quinoa.
- Produce: berries, pears, spinach, cucumber, tomato, broccoli, carrots, potatoes, green beans, sweet potato, avocado, and lemons.
- Pantry: olive oil, pepper, dried herbs, and spices. Check dressings and canned sauces for added sugar.

This list gives ingredient types rather than a fixed household quantity. Count servings from the menu before buying, especially if cooking for more than one person.

## Prep and storage

Cook grains and lentils for the first three days, freeze later portions, and plan a Wednesday top-up. Keep raw chicken and fish refrigerated for only their safe raw storage window or freeze them. Cooked leftovers generally keep 3–4 days refrigerated at 40°F or below; reheat to 165°F.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does no added sugar mean no carbohydrates?** No. Oats, beans, fruit, and potatoes contain carbohydrates and remain part of the menu.

**Can food replace treatment for inflammatory disease?** No. Discuss dietary changes with your clinician, especially if you have restrictions, allergies, or medication considerations.
