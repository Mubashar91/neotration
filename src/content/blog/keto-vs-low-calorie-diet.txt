## What Is the Keto Diet?

The ketogenic diet is a very low-carbohydrate, high-fat eating plan. The goal is to get your body into a state called ketosis, where it burns fat for fuel instead of carbohydrates.

A standard keto macro split looks like this:

- Fat: 70–75% of daily calories
- Protein: 20–25% of daily calories
- Carbohydrates: 5% of daily calories (typically under 25–50g per day)

This means avoiding bread, pasta, rice, most fruits, and any sugar. Instead, keto dieters eat meat, fish, eggs, cheese, nuts, avocado, butter, and non-starchy vegetables.

## What Is a Low Calorie Diet?

A low-calorie diet simply means eating fewer calories than your body burns each day — creating a calorie deficit. There are no forbidden foods. You can eat carbs, fat, and protein in any combination you like, as long as your total daily calories stay within your target range.

## Keto vs Low Calorie: Head-to-Head Comparison

### Weight Loss Speed

Keto often produces faster initial weight loss — but much of this is water weight, not fat. Studies show that over 6–12 months, both approaches produce similar fat loss results when calories are controlled.

### Hunger and Cravings

Many people find keto more satisfying because fat and protein are very filling. However, giving up carbs can cause intense cravings in the first 1–2 weeks (known as the "keto flu").

### Ease of Following

A low-calorie diet is more flexible and easier to maintain long-term. You can eat out at restaurants, attend social events, and enjoy a wider variety of foods.

### Health Benefits Beyond Weight Loss

Keto has shown benefits for blood sugar control and may be helpful for people with Type 2 diabetes or insulin resistance. A low-calorie diet improves overall health markers when it includes a wide variety of whole foods.

### Cost

Keto can be more expensive due to high consumption of meat, fish, and specialty products. A low-calorie diet can be done affordably with beans, oats, eggs, and vegetables.

### Sustainability

Most research suggests low-calorie diets are easier to maintain long-term. Many people try keto, lose weight, then regain it when they reintroduce carbs.

## What Do US Nutritionists Recommend?

Most registered dietitians in the United States do not recommend keto as a first-line approach for the general population. While it works well for some people — especially those with metabolic conditions — it is highly restrictive and difficult to sustain.

The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics and most US health organizations recommend a balanced, calorie-controlled diet rich in whole foods, lean protein, fruits, vegetables, and whole grains as the safest and most effective long-term approach.

That said, if keto fits your lifestyle and you enjoy it, it can absolutely work. The best diet is the one you can actually stick to.

## Which Diet Should You Choose?

Choose keto if: You love high-fat foods, don't mind giving up carbs, have insulin resistance or Type 2 diabetes, and want faster initial results.

Choose a low-calorie diet if: You want flexibility, enjoy a wide variety of foods, eat out frequently, or are looking for a long-term sustainable lifestyle change.

The bottom line: Both diets work because both ultimately create a calorie deficit. Keto does it by eliminating an entire food group. A low-calorie diet does it by portion control. The science shows similar results — so choose the approach that fits your life.
