## Animal-Based Protein Sources

### 1. Chicken breast
31g protein per 3.5 oz (cooked). The gold standard of lean protein. Versatile, affordable, and widely available.

### 2. Turkey breast
29g per 3.5 oz. Slightly leaner than chicken with a similar protein content.

### 3. Tuna (canned in water)
25g per 3.5 oz. One of the most affordable high-protein foods in America. Great for quick lunches.

### 4. Salmon
25g per 3.5 oz plus heart-healthy omega-3 fatty acids.

### 5. Shrimp
24g per 3.5 oz. Low calorie, high protein, quick to cook.

### 6. Tilapia
23g per 3.5 oz. Budget-friendly white fish with very little fat.

### 7. Lean ground beef (93% lean)
22g per 3.5 oz. Great for meal prep — cook in bulk and use all week.

### 8. Pork tenderloin
22g per 3.5 oz. One of the leanest cuts of pork.

### 9. Eggs
6g per large egg. Extremely nutritious and affordable. Two eggs give you 12g of complete protein.

### 10. Egg whites
11g per 3.5 oz with almost zero fat. Great for high-volume, low-calorie protein.

## Dairy-Based Protein Sources

### 11. Greek yogurt (non-fat)
17g per 6 oz serving. Also high in calcium and probiotics.

### 12. Cottage cheese (low-fat)
14g per ½ cup. Slow-digesting casein protein — excellent before bed.

### 13. Low-fat milk
8g per cup. Easy way to add protein to oats, smoothies, and coffee.

### 14. Skyr (Icelandic yogurt)
17g per 5 oz. Similar to Greek yogurt, now widely available in US grocery stores.

### 15. Part-skim ricotta
14g per ½ cup. Great in pasta dishes or as a savory snack.

## Plant-Based Protein Sources

### 16. Edamame
17g per cup. One of the best plant proteins — complete amino acid profile.

### 17. Lentils
18g per cup (cooked). High in protein and fiber. Great for soups, salads, and curries.

### 18. Black beans
15g per cup (cooked). Budget-friendly and versatile.

### 19. Chickpeas
15g per cup (cooked). Great roasted as a snack or added to salads.

### 20. Tofu (firm)
10g per 3.5 oz. Absorbs flavors well — excellent in stir-fries.

### 21. Tempeh
19g per 3.5 oz. Fermented soy with even more protein than tofu.

### 22. Seitan
25g per 3.5 oz. Made from wheat gluten — one of the highest plant protein sources.

### 23. Quinoa
8g per cup (cooked). A complete protein grain — use it instead of rice.

## Protein Supplements

### 24. Whey protein powder
20–25g per scoop. Fast-digesting — ideal after workouts.

### 25. Casein protein powder
20–24g per scoop. Slow-digesting — ideal before bed.

### 26. Plant-based protein powder
15–20g per scoop. Good option for those avoiding dairy.

## Convenient High-Protein Snacks

### 27. String cheese
6–8g per stick. Easy, portable protein snack.

### 28. Jerky (beef or turkey)
10–15g per oz. High-protein snack for on the go.

### 29. Deli turkey slices
10g per 2 oz. Quick protein without cooking.

### 30. Protein bars
15–25g depending on brand. Convenient but check sugar content.

## Tips for Hitting Your Protein Target

Include a protein source at every meal and snack. Prep proteins in bulk on Sundays. Add Greek yogurt or cottage cheese to smoothies. Use protein powder in oatmeal or pancakes. Choose high-protein options when eating out — grilled chicken, steak, or fish.

## Final Thoughts

Eating enough protein is one of the most powerful things you can do for your body composition. With this list, you have 30 options to mix and match across every meal.
