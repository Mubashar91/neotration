## 1. You're Eating More Calories Than You Think

This is the number one reason people stop losing weight. Research consistently shows that people underestimate their calorie intake by 20–40%. Restaurant portions are bigger than they look, cooking oils add up fast, and those "small bites" while cooking count too.

Fix: Use a food scale and track every calorie for at least two weeks. Use the FitJourney USA calculator to confirm your daily calorie target, then track against it precisely.

## 2. You've Lost Weight and Haven't Adjusted Your Calories

As you lose weight, your body burns fewer calories. A calorie deficit that worked when you weighed 200 pounds may no longer be a deficit at 180 pounds.

Fix: Recalculate your TDEE every 4–6 weeks using the FitJourney USA calculator and adjust your intake accordingly.

## 3. You're Not Eating Enough Protein

Low protein intake leads to muscle loss during a calorie deficit. Less muscle means a slower metabolism — meaning you burn fewer calories at rest.

Fix: Aim for 0.7–1 gram of protein per pound of body weight daily. Prioritize protein at every meal.

## 4. You're Drinking Hidden Calories

A daily latte, a glass of wine with dinner, a sports drink after a workout — these add up to hundreds of calories that most people don't account for.

Fix: Track all beverages. Stick to water, black coffee, and unsweetened tea as your primary drinks.

## 5. You're Not Sleeping Enough

Sleep deprivation disrupts hunger hormones and slows metabolism. People who sleep less than 6 hours per night consistently lose less fat and more muscle than those who sleep 7–9 hours.

Fix: Prioritize 7–9 hours of sleep. Create a bedtime routine and stick to a consistent sleep schedule.

## 6. You're Too Stressed

Chronic stress raises cortisol, which promotes fat storage — especially belly fat — and increases cravings for high-calorie foods.

Fix: Add stress management practices to your routine: daily walks, meditation, journaling, or yoga. Even 10 minutes makes a difference.

## 7. You're Doing Too Much Cardio and Not Enough Strength Training

Excessive cardio without strength training can cause muscle loss, which lowers your metabolism over time.

Fix: Replace some cardio sessions with 2–3 strength training sessions per week. Building muscle increases the number of calories you burn at rest.

## 8. You're Eating "Healthy" Foods That Are High in Calories

Avocado, nuts, olive oil, granola, and whole grain bread are all healthy — but they're also high in calories. Eating them in large quantities can easily erase your calorie deficit.

Fix: Measure portions of calorie-dense healthy foods. A serving of almonds is about 23 nuts — not the whole bag.

## 9. You're Not Drinking Enough Water

Mild dehydration can slow your metabolism and be mistaken for hunger, leading to unnecessary snacking.

Fix: Drink at least 8–10 cups of water per day. Drink a full glass before each meal to reduce appetite.

## 10. You're Experiencing a True Plateau

After several weeks of consistent progress, your body adapts and weight loss naturally slows. This is a physiological plateau, not a failure.

Fix: Break the plateau by changing your workout routine, taking a 1–2 week diet break at maintenance calories, or slightly increasing your deficit. Small changes are often enough to restart progress.

## Final Thoughts

If you're not losing weight, something in your approach needs adjusting — not abandoning. Work through this list methodically and identify which factors apply to you.
