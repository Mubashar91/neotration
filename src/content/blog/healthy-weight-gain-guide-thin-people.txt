# Healthy Weight Gain: A Complete Guide for Naturally Thin People

Many people focus on losing weight, but gaining weight healthily can be just as challenging. If you struggle to put on pounds, this guide is for you.

## What Healthy Weight Gain Means
Healthy weight gain means building muscle and fueling your body — not just eating junk food. The goal is to increase calories with nutrient-dense foods while supporting muscle growth and recovery.

## Increase Your Calorie Intake — Smartly
Add 300–500 extra calories daily above maintenance. Choose calorie-dense foods:
- Nuts, nut butters, trail mix
- Avocados and olive oil
- Whole grains (oats, rice, pasta)
- Dried fruit and full‑fat dairy
- Smoothies with milk, oats, peanut butter

## Eat More Frequently
Instead of three large meals, aim for 5–6 smaller meals. Snack between meals. Don’t skip meals — set reminders if appetite is low.

Sample day:
- Breakfast: Oats with milk, banana, peanut butter
- Snack: Trail mix + yogurt
- Lunch: Chicken rice bowl with avocado
- Snack: Smoothie (milk, whey, oats, cocoa)
- Dinner: Pasta with meat sauce + olive oil drizzle
- Snack: Cottage cheese + fruit

## Prioritize Protein
Include protein every time you eat:
- Chicken, beef, fish, eggs
- Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, milk
- Beans, lentils, tofu, whey

Target: ~0.7–1.0 g protein per lb bodyweight (or 1.6–2.2 g/kg).

## Strength Training Is Essential
Lift 3–4 days/week focusing on compound moves:
- Squat, deadlift, bench, row, overhead press
- 3–4 sets of 6–12 reps, progressive overload

Keep cardio light to avoid burning your surplus. Walk for health.

## Recovery, Sleep, and Consistency
Aim for 7–9 hours sleep. Manage stress. Track bodyweight 2–3x/week and adjust calories by +100–200 if weight doesn’t rise after 2 weeks.

## Example 3‑Day Split
- Day A: Squat, bench, row, accessory
- Day B: Deadlift, overhead press, pull‑ups, accessory
- Day C: Front squat, incline bench, RDL, accessory

## Bottom Line
Be patient (0.5–1 lb per week). Eat consistently, lift hard, and choose calorie‑dense, nutritious foods. Your frame will fill out — steadily and healthily.
