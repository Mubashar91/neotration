The Mediterranean diet is one of the most researched eating patterns in the world. It is consistently linked with better heart health, and US News & World Report has ranked it the #1 overall diet for years running. The good news for beginners is that it isn't a strict diet at all. It's a way of building plates around vegetables, olive oil, beans, whole grains, fish, and a little cheese and yogurt.

This guide explains the basics in plain terms, then gives you a simple 7-day starter plan with approximate calories and protein for each day.

## What the Mediterranean Diet Actually Is

The pattern is based on how people traditionally ate in countries such as Greece, southern Italy, and Spain. There's no single official version, but the core principles are consistent:

**Eat most days:**
- Vegetables and fruit (aim for color at every meal)
- Whole grains: oats, whole-wheat bread, farro, brown rice, bulgur
- Beans, lentils, and chickpeas
- Extra-virgin olive oil as your main fat
- Nuts, seeds, herbs, and spices

**Eat a few times a week:**
- Fish and seafood (2+ times a week)
- Poultry, eggs, yogurt, and cheese in moderate portions

**Eat occasionally:**
- Red meat, sweets, and highly processed foods

## Is It Good for Calorie Control?

It can be. Mediterranean meals are high in fiber and fill you up well, but olive oil and nuts are calorie-dense: 1 tbsp of olive oil is about 120 calories. Measure oil for the first couple of weeks until you can eyeball it.

To find a daily calorie target that fits your goals, use the [FitJourney USA Calorie Calculator](/calculator). The plan below averages about **1,600–1,800 calories and 85–100g protein per day**. Adjust portions up or down to match your number.

## Mediterranean Pantry Starter List

- Extra-virgin olive oil
- Canned chickpeas, lentils, and white beans
- Canned tuna or salmon
- Rolled oats, whole-wheat pita, farro or bulgur
- Plain Greek yogurt and feta cheese
- Frozen spinach, cherry tomatoes, cucumbers, onions, garlic, lemons
- Almonds or walnuts
- Dried oregano, cumin, paprika

## The 7-Day Mediterranean Starter Plan

### Day 1 (~1,650 cal | 92g protein)
- **Breakfast:** Greek yogurt (1 cup) with berries, walnuts (1 tbsp), and honey
- **Lunch:** Chickpea salad with cucumber, tomato, red onion, feta, olive oil and lemon
- **Snack:** Apple + 15 almonds
- **Dinner:** Baked salmon (5 oz), roasted zucchini, ½ cup farro

### Day 2 (~1,700 cal | 88g protein)
- **Breakfast:** Oats cooked in milk with sliced banana and cinnamon
- **Lunch:** Leftover salmon over greens with olive oil and lemon dressing
- **Snack:** Hummus (¼ cup) with carrots and bell pepper
- **Dinner:** Chicken souvlaki (5 oz chicken) with tzatziki, tomato-cucumber salad, half a whole-wheat pita

### Day 3 (~1,620 cal | 85g protein)
- **Breakfast:** 2-egg omelet with spinach, tomato and feta; 1 slice whole-grain toast
- **Lunch:** Lentil soup (2 cups) with a side salad
- **Snack:** Greek yogurt with a drizzle of honey
- **Dinner:** Shrimp (5 oz) sautéed with garlic, cherry tomatoes, and whole-wheat spaghetti (1 cup cooked)

### Day 4 (~1,750 cal | 95g protein)
- **Breakfast:** Greek yogurt parfait with oats and berries
- **Lunch:** Tuna, white bean and arugula salad with olive oil and lemon
- **Snack:** Orange + a small handful of walnuts
- **Dinner:** Sheet-pan chicken thighs with potatoes, red onion, and oregano

### Day 5 (~1,680 cal | 90g protein)
- **Breakfast:** Whole-grain toast with mashed avocado, tomato, and a boiled egg
- **Lunch:** Leftover sheet-pan chicken over greens
- **Snack:** Cottage cheese with cucumber and cherry tomatoes
- **Dinner:** Baked cod with lemon, green beans, and ½ cup bulgur

### Day 6 (~1,800 cal | 98g protein)
- **Breakfast:** Shakshuka (2 eggs poached in spiced tomato sauce) with half a pita
- **Lunch:** Greek grain bowl: farro, chickpeas, cucumber, olives, feta, tzatziki
- **Snack:** Pear + 1 string cheese
- **Dinner:** Turkey meatballs (5 oz) in marinara with roasted eggplant

### Day 7 (~1,620 cal | 86g protein)
- **Breakfast:** Overnight oats with Greek yogurt, chia seeds, and berries
- **Lunch:** Leftover meatballs with a big green salad
- **Snack:** Hummus with whole-grain crackers
- **Dinner:** Grilled fish or tofu with a large Mediterranean chopped salad and lemon-herb potatoes

## 5 Beginner Tips That Make It Stick

1. **Change one meal at a time.** Start with lunch salads or a fish night rather than overhauling everything on Monday.
2. **Cook once, eat twice.** Most dinners above double as the next day's lunch.
3. **Use canned beans and fish.** They're cheap, fast, and very much in the spirit of the diet.
4. **Measure olive oil at first.** "A drizzle" can easily become 3–4 tablespoons.
5. **Make flavor your focus.** Lemon, garlic, herbs, and spices are what make vegetables something you look forward to.

## Common Questions

**Is the Mediterranean diet expensive?** Not necessarily. Beans, lentils, oats, frozen vegetables, and canned fish are some of the cheapest whole foods in a US grocery store. See our [high fiber foods list](/blog/high-fiber-foods-list) for affordable staples.

**Can I drink wine?** Traditionally, wine is part of the pattern in moderation, but health organizations don't recommend starting to drink for health benefits. It's optional.

## The Bottom Line

The Mediterranean diet works because it is flexible and satisfying. Follow this 7-day plan as a template, swap in foods you like, and keep the core habits: vegetables at every meal, olive oil as your fat, fish twice a week, and beans often.
