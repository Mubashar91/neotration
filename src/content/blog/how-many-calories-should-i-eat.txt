## What Is a Calorie?

A calorie is a unit of energy. Your body uses calories from food to power every function — from breathing and circulation to exercise and digestion. When you eat more calories than your body needs, the excess is stored as fat. When you eat fewer, your body burns stored fat for energy.

## Two Numbers You Need to Know

### Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)

The number of calories your body burns at complete rest — just to keep your organs functioning. This accounts for about 60–70% of your total daily calorie burn.

### Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

Your BMR multiplied by your activity level. This is the total number of calories your body burns in a full day, including all movement and exercise. Your TDEE is your maintenance number — the calories needed to stay at your current weight.

As a general reference, here are average TDEE ranges for American adults:

- Sedentary women (little to no exercise): 1,600–1,800 calories/day
- Active women (exercise 3–5 days/week): 1,900–2,200 calories/day
- Sedentary men: 2,000–2,400 calories/day
- Active men: 2,400–3,000 calories/day

## How Many Calories to Eat for Your Goal

Once you know your TDEE, adjusting for your goal is straightforward:

For weight loss: Eat 300–500 calories below your TDEE. This creates a moderate deficit that leads to 0.5–1 pound of fat loss per week — the safest, most sustainable rate.

For muscle gain: Eat 200–300 calories above your TDEE. This gives your body the energy it needs to build new muscle tissue without excessive fat gain.

For maintenance: Eat at your TDEE. This is ideal when you're happy with your weight and just want to stay healthy and energized.

## Why Calorie Needs Are Different for Everyone

Two people of the same height and weight can have very different calorie needs based on age (metabolism slows with age), muscle mass (muscle burns more calories than fat), activity level, genetics, and hormonal factors.

This is why generic advice like "eat 2,000 calories a day" doesn't work for everyone. Personalized calculation is always more accurate.

## Do Calories from Different Foods Matter?

While total calories determine weight change, the quality of those calories affects everything else — your energy, hunger, muscle, and health. 1,500 calories of whole foods (protein, vegetables, whole grains) will leave you fuller, more energized, and healthier than 1,500 calories of processed snacks, even though the scale effect is the same.

Focus on hitting your calorie target with mostly whole, nutrient-dense foods for the best results.

## How Often Should You Recalculate?

Recalculate your TDEE every 4–6 weeks, especially if you're losing or gaining weight. As your body weight changes, so does your calorie need. Failing to adjust is one of the most common reasons people hit a weight loss plateau.

## Final Thoughts

Your daily calorie target is the foundation of every nutrition goal. Get this number right and everything else — food choices, meal timing, macros — becomes a refinement on top of a solid base.
