Dense bean salad is a no-cook lunch built from canned beans, chopped vegetables, a dressing, and an optional extra protein. Here are five two-serving combinations, plus a prep schedule that keeps a workweek practical: make one batch on Sunday and another on Wednesday rather than assuming every salad lasts seven days.

Use drained, rinsed beans and cooked or ready-to-eat proteins. A 15 oz can usually gives about 1 1/2 cups drained beans, but check the actual yield. The recipes below use 1 cup per batch, leaving some beans for another meal.

## The basic two-lunch formula

Mix 1 cup drained beans with 2 cups chopped vegetables, 6 oz cooked chicken or 8 oz ready-to-eat tofu, 1 tbsp olive oil, and 2 tbsp lemon juice or vinegar. Divide into two containers. This is a starting portion; add a roll, rice, or extra vegetables according to appetite.

Beans supply fiber as well as some protein. Chicken, tofu, tuna, eggs, or edamame can increase protein, but none is interchangeable gram for gram. Use product labels to calculate nutrition; a salad containing beans is not automatically a 30g-protein meal.

## Five dense bean salads

1. **Lemon chickpea chicken:** Combine 1 cup chickpeas, 6 oz chopped cooked chicken, 1 cup cucumber, 1 cup tomato, 1 tbsp olive oil, 2 tbsp lemon juice, and 2 tbsp parsley. Toss, chill, and divide into two lunches. Add cucumber shortly before eating for more crunch.
2. **Southwest black bean tofu:** Mix 1 cup black beans, 8 oz ready-to-eat baked tofu, 1 cup diced pepper, 1/2 cup corn, 1/2 cup tomato, 1 tbsp olive oil, 2 tbsp lime juice, and 1/2 tsp cumin. Check tofu packaging: some products require cooking first.
3. **White bean tuna:** Drain one 5 oz can light tuna. Fold it into 1 cup cannellini beans, 1 cup celery, 1 cup cucumber, 1 tbsp olive oil, 2 tbsp lemon juice, and 1 tsp mustard. Divide between two containers; pack whole-grain crackers separately.
4. **Lentil edamame crunch:** Use 1 cup cooked cooled lentils, 1 cup cooked cooled shelled edamame, 1 cup shredded carrot, and 1 cup cabbage. Dress with 1 tbsp olive oil, 2 tbsp rice vinegar, and 1 tsp reduced-sodium soy sauce. Refrigerate promptly.
5. **Greek-style butter bean:** Combine 1 cup butter beans, 8 oz ready-to-eat tofu, 1 cup cucumber, 1 cup tomato, 2 tbsp crumbled feta, 1 tbsp olive oil, 2 tbsp red-wine vinegar, and oregano. Olives are optional; account for them separately if tracking nutrition.

## Shopping list for two batches

- Two 15 oz cans of your chosen beans or cooked lentils.
- Two protein choices: cooked chicken, packaged ready-to-eat tofu, canned tuna, or cooked edamame.
- Four cups chopped vegetables per batch; buying peppers, cucumber, carrots, and cabbage allows overlap.
- Olive oil, lemons or vinegar, herbs, mustard, and seasonings.
- Two to four lunch containers, plus small pots for crackers or dressing.

Buy for the recipes you will actually make. Making all five at once gives ten lunches and creates a storage problem for one person.

## Storage and work lunches

Keep prepared salads at 40°F or below. Use a conservative 3–4-day window for mixed meal-prep leftovers and follow any shorter product instructions. Use an insulated bag with cold packs when a refrigerator is unavailable. Acidic dressing does not make chicken, tuna, or tofu shelf-stable.

These salads are best freshly assembled rather than frozen: cucumber and tomato lose texture. Freeze extra cooked beans separately if needed. ## Frequently asked questions

**Can I make it vegetarian?** Yes. Choose tofu or edamame and omit meat and fish. Read labels for protein amounts and preparation instructions.

**Will dressing preserve it all week?** No. A vinaigrette adds flavor; it does not replace refrigeration or safe storage limits.
