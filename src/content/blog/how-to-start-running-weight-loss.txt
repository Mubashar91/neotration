## How Many Calories Does Running Burn?

Running burns more calories per minute than almost any other common exercise. A 160-pound person burns approximately 300–400 calories in 30 minutes of running at a moderate pace (5–6 mph). At a faster pace, that number climbs higher.

Combined with the right calorie intake from the FitJourney USA calculator, running can accelerate weight loss significantly.

## Before You Start: Set Your Calorie Target

Running burns calories — but if you eat back everything you burn, you won't lose weight. Before your first run, use the FitJourney USA calorie calculator to set your daily intake goal. Keep that target consistent whether or not you exercise that day. Let the running create additional deficit on top of your dietary baseline.

## The Run-Walk Method for Beginners

The biggest mistake new runners make is going too hard too soon. They sprint until they're gasping for air, feel terrible, and never run again. The run-walk method prevents this by alternating running and walking intervals.

- Week 1–2: Run 1 minute, walk 2 minutes. Repeat 8 times (24 minutes total). Do this 3 days per week.
- Week 3–4: Run 2 minutes, walk 1 minute. Repeat 7 times.
- Week 5–6: Run 5 minutes, walk 1 minute. Repeat 4 times.
- Week 7–8: Run 10 minutes, walk 1 minute. Repeat 3 times.
- Week 9–10: Run 20 minutes continuously.
- Week 11–12: Run 30 minutes continuously.

By following this progression, most beginners can run 30 minutes continuously within 12 weeks — with much lower injury risk than jumping straight into running.

## How Often Should You Run?

For weight loss, aim for 3–4 running sessions per week with at least one rest day between sessions. Your muscles and joints need time to recover, especially in the early weeks. More is not always better — overtraining leads to injury and burnout.

## What to Eat Before and After Running

For runs under 45 minutes, you don't need to eat beforehand — especially if you run in the morning. For longer runs, eat a light carbohydrate snack 30–60 minutes before: a banana, a slice of toast with peanut butter, or a small bowl of oats.

After your run, eat a protein-rich meal within 60–90 minutes to support muscle recovery. Eggs, Greek yogurt, chicken with rice, or a protein shake all work well.

## Running Tips for Beginners

Run at a pace where you can hold a conversation — if you can't speak in sentences, slow down.

Invest in proper running shoes from a specialty running store.

Run on softer surfaces like grass or trails when possible to reduce joint impact.

Warm up with 5 minutes of brisk walking before every run.

Cool down and stretch your calves, hamstrings, and hip flexors after.

## Combine Running With Strength Training

Running is excellent cardio, but adding 2 strength training sessions per week produces dramatically better body composition results than running alone. Strength training preserves muscle mass while you lose fat, giving you a leaner, more toned appearance rather than simply being a smaller version of yourself.

## Final Thoughts

Running is one of the most accessible and effective tools for weight loss. Start slow, be consistent, and combine it with a proper calorie plan for the best results. Most people are surprised how quickly their fitness improves in the first few weeks.
