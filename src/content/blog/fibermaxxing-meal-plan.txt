This seven-day fibermaxxing meal plan combines beans, whole grains, fruit, vegetables, and protein foods without turning fiber into a competition. The sample portions provide roughly 30g or more of fiber on many days, but the goal is a comfortable, varied eating pattern rather than the largest possible number.

Increase fiber gradually if you currently eat little of it. NIDDK describes adult fiber needs as about 22–34g a day depending on age and sex and recommends adding it a little at a time. Follow your clinician's advice if you have a digestive condition or have been told to limit fiber.

## The meal-building portions

These are meal components, not a calorie prescription. Add enough food for your energy needs. Nutrition varies by brand, portion, and preparation; the figures below are rounded fiber estimates based on generic foods, not laboratory analysis.

- Oat breakfast: 1/2 cup dry oats, 1 tbsp chia seeds, 1/2 cup raspberries, and 3/4 cup plain Greek yogurt. About 12g fiber; yogurt supplies the main protein.
- Lentil lunch: 3/4 cup cooked lentils, 1/2 cup cooked quinoa, 1 cup chopped cucumber and tomato, lemon, and 1 tsp olive oil. About 14g fiber. Add tofu, chicken, or more yogurt if wanted.
- Bean bowl: 1/2 cup black beans, 1/2 cup cooked brown rice, 1 cup cooked broccoli, and 4 oz tofu or 3 oz cooked chicken. About 12g fiber.
- Salmon dinner: 4 oz salmon, 1 small skin-on potato, and 1 cup green beans. About 7g fiber.
- Snack: 1 medium pear with 1 oz almonds. About 9g fiber; halve the nut portion if that suits you better.

## Seven-day rotation

| Day | Breakfast | Lunch | Dinner | Fiber planning note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Oat breakfast | Lentil lunch | Salmon dinner | About 33g before snacks |
| Tuesday | Yogurt, pear, 1 tbsp chia | Lentil lunch | Bean bowl | Use a smaller lentil portion if needed |
| Wednesday | Oat breakfast | Chicken whole-grain sandwich with carrots | Bean bowl | Choose bread with fiber on its label |
| Thursday | Oat breakfast | Lentil lunch | Salmon dinner | Repeat Monday to simplify prep |
| Friday | Yogurt, pear, chia | Chicken sandwich and carrots | Bean bowl | Add fruit if intake is low |
| Saturday | Oat breakfast | Lentil lunch | Salmon dinner | Cook a fresh small batch |
| Sunday | Oats, yogurt, raspberries | Bean bowl | Salmon dinner | Add a pear snack if wanted |

## Shopping list

- Dry goods: oats, chia seeds, quinoa, brown rice, whole-grain bread, and almonds.
- Legumes: enough cooked lentils for four 3/4-cup portions and black beans for four 1/2-cup portions; canned, drained beans are convenient.
- Protein: plain Greek yogurt, tofu or chicken, and four salmon portions.
- Produce: raspberries, pears, potatoes, broccoli, green beans, carrots, cucumber, tomato, and lemons. Use frozen berries and vegetables as needed.
- Dressing: olive oil, lemon juice, pepper, and herbs. Check prepared sauces separately.

## A realistic prep routine

Cook lentils and grains while roasting potatoes. Portion the first three days, cool promptly, and freeze later cooked portions. Rinse canned beans if you prefer their flavor that way. Keep cucumber and tomato separate from hot ingredients until everything has cooled.

Refrigerate cooked leftovers at 40°F or below and use within 3–4 days. Plan a midweek top-up rather than keeping Sunday's cooked food until the following Sunday. The printable lentil bowl below makes two lunches.

## Make the plan easier on your stomach

Start with 1/4 cup beans instead of 1/2 cup, choose cooked vegetables, and spread fiber across meals. Have fluids according to your needs and care plan. More fiber is not always better, and severe or persistent symptoms deserve medical advice.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a fiber supplement?** This plan uses food. Ask a clinician about supplements, especially if you take medicines or have digestive symptoms.

**Can I make it dairy-free?** Use an unsweetened fortified soy yogurt and check its protein content; many coconut yogurts provide very little protein.
