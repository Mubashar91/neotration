Five-ingredient dinners reduce decisions between getting home and eating. These twelve single-serving meals each use five main ingredients. Water, salt, pepper, and dried seasonings are pantry basics; oil counts when used.

Most take about 15–30 minutes with precooked grains, beans, and chicken. Starting with raw rice or dry beans changes the timing. These are higher-protein ideas, not a promise that every version provides one fixed amount.

## Twelve dinners with five main ingredients

1. **Chicken broccoli rice:** Use 4 oz cooked chicken, 1/2 cup cooked rice, 1 cup broccoli, 1 tsp oil, and 1 tbsp lemon juice. Cook broccoli in oil and water, add chicken and rice, and heat leftovers to 165°F.
2. **Tuna tomato pasta:** Use one drained 5 oz can light tuna, 2 oz dry whole-grain pasta, 1/2 cup tomatoes, 1 cup spinach, and 1 tsp oil. Boil pasta; warm sauce ingredients and toss together.
3. **Egg bean skillet:** Use 2 eggs, 3/4 cup black beans, 1/2 cup tomatoes, 1 cup spinach, and 1 tsp oil. Warm beans and vegetables; add eggs, cover, and cook until whites and yolks are firm.
4. **Tofu edamame:** Use 6 oz firm tofu, 1/2 cup cooked edamame, 1 cup broccoli, 1 tbsp reduced-sodium soy sauce, and 1 tsp oil. Cook tofu and broccoli; add edamame and sauce and heat through.
5. **Salmon couscous:** Use 5 oz raw salmon, 1/3 cup dry couscous, 1 cup peas, 1 tbsp lemon juice, and 1 tsp oil. Prepare couscous and peas; pan-cook salmon to 145°F and finish with lemon.
6. **Turkey white bean:** Use 5 oz raw ground turkey, 1/2 cup white beans, 1/2 cup tomatoes, 1 cup zucchini, and 1 tsp oil. Brown turkey; add remaining foods and simmer to 165°F.
7. **Chicken hummus pita:** Use 4 oz cooked chicken, 1 whole-grain pita, 3 tbsp hummus, 1/2 cup cucumber, and 1/2 cup tomato. Fill pita cold with safely chilled chicken or reheat chicken to 165°F first.
8. **Shrimp pea pasta:** Use 6 oz peeled raw shrimp, 2 oz dry pasta, 1 cup peas, 1 tsp oil, and 1 tbsp lemon juice. Boil pasta and peas; sauté shrimp until pearly and opaque, then combine.
9. **Lentil cottage potato:** Use 1 small potato, 3/4 cup cooked lentils, 1/2 cup cottage cheese, 1/2 cup salsa, and 1 cup spinach. Cook potato until tender, warm lentils and spinach, then add toppings.
10. **Chicken corn soup:** Use 4 oz cooked chicken, 1/2 cup corn, 1/2 cup white beans, 1 cup low-sodium broth, and 1/2 cup tomatoes. Simmer until leftovers reach 165°F.
11. **Peanut tofu noodles:** Use 6 oz tofu, 2 oz dry noodles, 1 cup cabbage, 1 tbsp peanut butter, and 1 tbsp soy sauce. Boil noodles; soften tofu and cabbage in water, then mix with sauce thinned with cooking water.
12. **Egg yogurt toast:** Use 3 eggs, 1 slice whole-grain toast, 1/2 cup Greek yogurt, 1 cup spinach, and 1 tsp oil. Cook spinach in oil and scramble eggs until firm. Serve with toast and yogurt.

## Shop for three meals first

Chicken broccoli rice, chicken hummus pita, and chicken corn soup share cooked chicken. Buy rice, pita, hummus, beans, frozen corn and broccoli, cucumber, tomatoes, broth, and lemon. You do not need all twelve recipes' ingredients at once.

Write down tonight's meal, tomorrow's meal, and a backup from the pantry. See the [budget worksheet](/blog/budget-high-protein-meal-prep), [freezer bags](/blog/freezer-meal-prep-weight-loss), and [January dinner rotation](/blog/january-reset-dinner-rotation) for longer plans.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do seasonings count?** Salt, pepper, dried spices, and water are pantry basics here. Sauces, oil, and dairy are counted. Check every ingredient for allergens.

**Can I store all twelve?** Cook small batches. Refrigerated cooked leftovers generally keep 3–4 days; freeze suitable later portions promptly and reheat to 165°F.
