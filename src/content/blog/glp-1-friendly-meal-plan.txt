Small, protein-first meals can make meal planning more manageable when appetite is low. This seven-day GLP-1-friendly menu offers four eating occasions a day, flexible portions, and a shopping list. It is a starting point to discuss with your prescriber or registered dietitian, not a prescription for a particular medication.

There is no single approved GLP-1 diet. This menu deliberately avoids a fixed calorie allowance and a universal protein target: your body size, activity, kidney function, other conditions, and treatment plan all matter. Add bread, rice, fruit, fats, or additional meals to meet your needs; these small meals are not necessarily a complete day's intake.

## Build four small meals from five templates

Use portions you can comfortably manage, then adjust with your care team. Start with a protein source, add an easily tolerated carbohydrate, and include produce. Foods described as healthy are not automatically comfortable during nausea.

- Yogurt bowl: 3/4 cup plain Greek yogurt, 1/4 cup oats, and 1/2 cup berries. Let the oats soften if preferred.
- Egg plate: 2 eggs, 1 slice whole-grain toast, and 1/2 cup cooked spinach. Cook eggs until firm.
- Chicken cup: 3 oz cooked chicken, 1/2 cup cooked rice, and 1/2 cup soft cooked carrots with 1 tsp olive oil.
- Fish plate: 3 oz cooked salmon, 1/2 cup mashed potato, and 1/2 cup cooked green beans.
- Tofu bowl: 4 oz firm tofu, 1/2 cup cooked rice, and 1/2 cup cooked zucchini; warm through with a splash of water.

## A seven-day menu

The snack column is another eating occasion, not a mandatory extra. Repeat comfortable foods rather than forcing variety during a difficult week. Each day includes protein at several meals; check package labels instead of assuming a brand-independent daily total.

| Day | Breakfast | Lunch | Dinner | Small snack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Yogurt bowl | Chicken cup | Fish plate | 1/2 cup cottage cheese |
| Tuesday | Egg plate | Tofu bowl | Chicken cup | Plain yogurt and banana |
| Wednesday | Yogurt bowl | Fish plate | Tofu bowl | Cottage cheese and crackers |
| Thursday | Egg plate | Chicken cup | Fish plate | Yogurt and soft fruit |
| Friday | Yogurt bowl | Tofu bowl | Chicken cup | Milk or fortified soy milk |
| Saturday | Egg plate | Fish plate | Tofu bowl | Cottage cheese and berries |
| Sunday | Yogurt bowl | Chicken cup | Fish plate | Yogurt and crackers |

## Shopping list for one person

- Protein: about 1 lb raw chicken breast, 1 1/4 lb raw salmon, 2 packages firm tofu (14 oz each), 6 eggs, one 32 oz tub plus one 16 oz tub plain Greek yogurt, and a 16 oz tub cottage cheese.
- Grains and starches: oats, rice, whole-grain bread, potatoes, and crackers. Buy enough for your chosen portions and any extra meals.
- Produce: berries, bananas or other soft fruit, carrots, zucchini, spinach, and green beans; frozen vegetables reduce waste.
- Pantry: olive oil and mild seasonings. Leave spicy sauces optional.

## Prep twice, rather than refrigerating a week's fish

Cook the first few days of rice and chicken, portion into shallow containers, and refrigerate promptly. Freeze later portions on prep day. Cook fish in smaller batches and thaw frozen portions in the refrigerator. Use refrigerated cooked leftovers within 3–4 days and reheat leftovers to 165°F.

## When food advice is not enough

Keep your prescriber involved if side effects prevent regular eating or drinking. Persistent vomiting, dehydration, or severe abdominal pain needs medical attention. Do not change a medication dose or use this plan to manage symptoms without clinical advice. Drink according to your own care plan; a generic fluid target may be unsuitable for some conditions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to eat 80–120g protein?** No. The joint advisory discusses protein adequacy, but a dietitian should individualize your target. This article does not promise a particular daily intake.

**Should I avoid all carbohydrates?** No. Rice, potatoes, oats, bread, and fruit can help supply energy. Adjust portions to your needs and tolerance.
