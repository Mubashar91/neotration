High-protein breakfast meal prep can be as simple as two egg-bite portions, two overnight-oat jars, and one yogurt jar. This five-day system uses three recipes and a midweek top-up, so Friday's breakfast is not cooked egg that has sat in the refrigerator since Sunday.

The portions below generally provide about 25–30g protein depending on your eggs, milk, yogurt, and cottage cheese. Figures are estimates; use package labels for precise tracking. Breakfast does not need one universal protein amount, and you can increase portions if you need more food.

## Five-day breakfast schedule

| Day | Breakfast | When to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Three egg bites and whole-grain toast | Bake Sunday |
| Tuesday | Overnight oats with yogurt | Mix Monday evening or Sunday |
| Wednesday | Three egg bites and toast | Sunday's batch, within 3–4 days |
| Thursday | Overnight oats with yogurt | Mix Wednesday evening |
| Friday | Greek yogurt berry jar | Assemble Thursday evening |

## Egg bites: two breakfasts

Whisk 4 large eggs with 1/2 cup low-fat cottage cheese and pepper. Divide 1 cup finely chopped spinach and peppers among six greased muffin cups, then pour in the egg mixture. Bake at 350°F for approximately 18–24 minutes until the center reaches 160°F. Check temperature rather than relying on the clock.

Serve three bites with one slice whole-grain toast. Typical labels give about 25–30g protein and roughly 300–380 calories including toast and a small amount of grease. Sausage and extra cheese change the total; neither is needed for the base.

Cool promptly, refrigerate covered, and use within 3–4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165°F. Freeze a portion promptly if you will eat it later, then thaw safely before reheating.

## Overnight oats: one jar

Stir together 1/2 cup rolled oats, 3/4 cup plain nonfat Greek yogurt, 1/2 cup low-fat milk, 1 tbsp chia seeds, and 1/2 cup berries. Cover and refrigerate at least four hours. Add a little more milk before eating if preferred.

Depending on the yogurt and milk, the jar provides roughly 25–30g protein and 380–450 calories. It needs no protein powder. Make jars at different points in the week rather than assuming seven-day storage. Use within 3–4 days and follow shorter dairy-label instructions.

## Yogurt jar: one breakfast

Layer 1 cup plain Greek yogurt with 1/2 cup berries and 2 tbsp pumpkin seeds. Keep 2 tbsp oats or low-sugar granola separate and add before eating. This generally supplies about 25–30g protein and 300–400 calories, varying by yogurt and granola.

For a dairy-free version, use unsweetened fortified soy yogurt and soy milk, then check protein. Coconut yogurt often supplies much less protein than dairy; substitutes are not automatically nutritionally identical.

## Shopping list for five breakfasts

- 4 large eggs and 1/2 cup cottage cheese.
- 2 1/2 cups plain Greek yogurt and 1 cup low-fat milk.
- 1 cup rolled oats, plus 2 tbsp oats or granola for Friday.
- 2 tbsp chia seeds and 2 tbsp pumpkin seeds.
- 1 1/2 cups berries and 1 cup chopped spinach and peppers.
- 2 slices whole-grain bread and a little oil for the tin.

Freeze spare bread and berries to limit waste. The recipe cards below repeat the cooking quantities for printing.

## A short prep routine

Start egg bites first. While they bake, measure oats into dry jars and wash produce. Mix only the first oat jar, refrigerate dairy, and leave a reminder for Wednesday's top-up. Keep crunchy toppings dry. Refrigerate perishable food promptly instead of leaving the entire prep on the counter until everything is done.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need protein powder?** No. These recipes use eggs, yogurt, cottage cheese, milk, oats, and seeds.

**Can I keep egg bites for five days?** Use a 3–4-day refrigerated leftovers window and freeze later portions promptly. Smell alone cannot confirm safety.
