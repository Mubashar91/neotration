Cottage cheese has quietly become one of the most popular high-protein foods in the US, and for good reason. A half-cup serving delivers 14 grams of protein for around 90 calories, making it one of the best protein-per-calorie foods available. If you're looking for cottage cheese recipes high protein enough to actually move the needle on your daily goals, this guide has 10 ideas with protein grams for each.

## Why Cottage Cheese Is a High-Protein Powerhouse

Cottage cheese is made from curds of pasteurized cow's milk. The protein is primarily casein — a slow-digesting protein that keeps you full for hours. Unlike Greek yogurt, cottage cheese has a milder flavor that works in both sweet and savory applications.

**Nutrition per ½ cup low-fat cottage cheese:**
- Calories: ~90
- Protein: 14g
- Carbs: 5g
- Fat: 2g

## 1. Cottage Cheese Scrambled Eggs (~28g protein)

Add ¼ cup cottage cheese to 3 beaten eggs before scrambling. The cottage cheese melts in and makes the eggs incredibly creamy while boosting protein significantly.

- 3 large eggs: 18g protein
- ¼ cup cottage cheese: 7g protein
- **Total: ~25–28g protein | ~250 calories**

Cook over medium-low heat, stirring gently. Season with salt, pepper, and fresh chives.

## 2. Cottage Cheese Pancakes (~22g protein per serving)

Blend ½ cup cottage cheese, 2 eggs, ¼ cup rolled oats, and ½ tsp vanilla until smooth. Cook like regular pancakes on a non-stick pan.

- Makes 4–5 small pancakes
- **~22g protein | ~280 calories per serving**

Top with fresh berries and a drizzle of honey instead of syrup to keep calories in check.

## 3. Savory Cottage Cheese Bowl (~20g protein)

Combine ½ cup cottage cheese with sliced cherry tomatoes, cucumber, a drizzle of olive oil, and everything bagel seasoning. Serve with whole grain crackers.

- **~20g protein | ~200 calories**

This takes 2 minutes to assemble and works as a quick lunch or snack.

## 4. Cottage Cheese Pasta Sauce (~25g protein per serving)

Blend 1 cup cottage cheese with 2 cloves garlic, 2 tbsp Parmesan, salt, and pepper until completely smooth. Toss with 2 cups cooked pasta and add cherry tomatoes and fresh basil.

- Serves 2
- **~25g protein per serving | ~420 calories**

The blended cottage cheese creates a creamy sauce that's indistinguishable from a heavy cream sauce at a fraction of the calories and fat.

## 5. Cottage Cheese Overnight Oats (~24g protein)

In a jar, combine ½ cup rolled oats, ½ cup cottage cheese, ½ cup unsweetened almond milk, 1 tbsp chia seeds, and ½ tsp vanilla. Stir well and refrigerate overnight. Top with berries in the morning.

- **~24g protein | ~350 calories**

The cottage cheese replaces Greek yogurt and adds a creamier texture with slightly more protein.

## 6. Cottage Cheese Dip with Vegetables (~16g protein)

Blend ½ cup cottage cheese with 1 tbsp lemon juice, 1 clove garlic, fresh dill, salt, and pepper. Serve with sliced bell peppers, carrots, and celery.

- **~16g protein | ~130 calories** (dip only)

This is a great alternative to hummus or ranch dip — higher protein, lower fat.

## 7. Cottage Cheese Stuffed Bell Peppers (~30g protein)

Mix 1 cup cottage cheese with ½ cup cooked quinoa, ¼ cup diced tomatoes, Italian seasoning, and salt. Stuff into 2 halved bell peppers. Bake at 375°F for 25 minutes. Top with 2 tbsp shredded mozzarella for the last 5 minutes.

- **~30g protein | ~380 calories per serving (2 halves)**

## 8. Cottage Cheese Smoothie (~30g protein)

Blend ½ cup cottage cheese, 1 cup frozen berries, ½ banana, ½ cup unsweetened almond milk, and 1 tbsp honey until smooth.

- **~30g protein | ~320 calories**

The cottage cheese adds creaminess and protein without a strong flavor. Most people can't tell it's in there.

## 9. Cottage Cheese Toast (~20g protein)

Spread ½ cup cottage cheese on 2 slices of whole grain toast. Top with sliced avocado, a sprinkle of red pepper flakes, and a drizzle of hot sauce.

- **~20g protein | ~350 calories**

A higher-protein alternative to avocado toast that keeps you full significantly longer.

## 10. Cottage Cheese Lasagna (~35g protein per serving)

Use cottage cheese instead of ricotta in your lasagna recipe. Mix 2 cups cottage cheese with 1 egg, ½ cup Parmesan, and Italian seasoning. Layer with whole wheat lasagna noodles, marinara, and lean ground beef or turkey.

- Serves 6
- **~35g protein per serving | ~420 calories**

This swap cuts fat significantly compared to full-fat ricotta while maintaining the creamy texture.

## Buying and Storing Cottage Cheese

Look for low-fat (2%) or non-fat cottage cheese for the best protein-to-calorie ratio. Full-fat versions have more calories but similar protein. Small curd and large curd have identical nutrition — it's purely a texture preference.

Cottage cheese keeps for 5–7 days after opening in the refrigerator. Don't freeze it — the texture becomes grainy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is cottage cheese good for weight loss?**
Cottage cheese is an excellent food for calorie-controlled eating. It's high in protein (which increases satiety), relatively low in calories, and versatile enough to use in dozens of recipes. Use the [FitJourney USA Daily Calorie Calculator](/daily-calorie) to see how it fits your plan.

**Q: How much protein is in cottage cheese?**
Low-fat cottage cheese provides approximately 14g of protein per ½ cup (113g). A full cup delivers ~28g of protein for ~180 calories.

**Q: Can I eat cottage cheese every day?**
Yes. Cottage cheese is a nutritious whole food. Eating it daily is safe and beneficial for most people. Vary your other protein sources to ensure a broad nutrient intake.

**Q: Is cottage cheese better than Greek yogurt for protein?**
They're comparable. Greek yogurt (non-fat) has ~17g protein per 6 oz vs. cottage cheese at ~14g per ½ cup. Greek yogurt is slightly higher in protein per serving, but cottage cheese is more versatile in savory cooking.

**Q: What does cottage cheese taste like?**
Plain cottage cheese has a mild, slightly tangy, creamy flavor. It's much milder than Greek yogurt. When blended, it becomes almost neutral — which is why it works so well in sauces, smoothies, and baked goods.
