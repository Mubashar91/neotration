## 1. Know Your Calorie Target

You can't out-exercise a bad diet. The foundation of every successful weight loss plan is a calorie deficit — eating fewer calories than your body burns each day. Before anything else, you need to know your number.

Use the free calorie calculator at FitJourney USA to find your personal daily calorie target based on your age, weight, height, and activity level. Once you have that number, everything else becomes much easier.

## 2. Eat More Protein at Every Meal

Protein is the most powerful nutrient for weight loss. It keeps you full longer, preserves muscle mass while you're in a deficit, and burns more calories during digestion than carbs or fat.

Aim for 0.7–1 gram of protein per pound of body weight daily. Great sources include chicken breast, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tuna, and lentils. Start every meal with your protein source and build around it.

## 3. Cut Liquid Calories First

One of the fastest ways to reduce your daily calorie intake is to stop drinking your calories. Sodas, fruit juices, energy drinks, flavored coffees, and alcohol can easily add 400–700 calories to your day without making you feel any fuller.

Switch to water, black coffee, unsweetened tea, or sparkling water. This single change alone has helped many Americans lose 10–15 pounds in a year without changing anything else.

## 4. Don't Skip Breakfast

People who eat a high-protein breakfast tend to eat fewer calories throughout the rest of the day. Skipping breakfast often leads to intense hunger by mid-morning, which makes you more likely to overeat at lunch or reach for processed snacks.

A good breakfast for weight loss includes eggs, oats with protein powder, or Greek yogurt with berries — all high in protein and fiber to keep you full until lunch.

## 5. Fill Half Your Plate With Vegetables

Non-starchy vegetables like broccoli, spinach, zucchini, peppers, and lettuce are extremely low in calories but high in fiber and water content. They fill your stomach, slow digestion, and reduce hunger — all without adding many calories to your meal.

A simple rule: fill half your plate with vegetables at lunch and dinner. You'll eat less of the higher-calorie foods without feeling deprived.

## 6. Eat Slowly and Mindfully

It takes about 20 minutes for your stomach to signal your brain that you're full. When you eat fast, you often consume far more than you need before that signal kicks in.

Put your fork down between bites, chew thoroughly, and avoid eating in front of a screen. Studies show that mindful eaters consume significantly fewer calories per meal without trying to restrict themselves.

## 7. Sleep 7–9 Hours Every Night

Sleep is one of the most underrated factors in weight loss. When you're sleep-deprived, your body produces more ghrelin (the hunger hormone) and less leptin (the fullness hormone). The result: you feel hungrier, crave high-calorie foods, and have less willpower to resist them.

Prioritizing 7–9 hours of quality sleep is one of the most powerful free tools for weight management available to you.

## 8. Walk at Least 8,000 Steps a Day

You don't need to spend hours at the gym to lose weight. Walking is one of the most effective and sustainable forms of exercise for fat loss. Aim for 8,000–10,000 steps per day, which burns an additional 300–500 calories depending on your body weight.

Take a walk after dinner, park farther away, take the stairs, or walk during phone calls. These small habits add up to significant calorie burn over weeks and months.

## 9. Meal Prep on Weekends

One of the biggest reasons people fall off their diet is convenience. When you're hungry and there's nothing healthy ready to eat, you reach for whatever is fast — and fast food is almost always high in calories.

Spending 2–3 hours on Sunday cooking proteins, chopping vegetables, and portioning snacks makes healthy eating effortless during the week. It removes decision fatigue and eliminates the temptation of fast food.

## 10. Track Your Progress Consistently

What gets measured gets managed. Weigh yourself once a week at the same time (first thing in the morning, after using the bathroom). Log your food daily. Take monthly progress photos.

Use the FitJourney USA nutrition calculator to track your calorie and macro intake. People who track consistently lose two to three times more weight than those who don't.

## Final Thoughts

Weight loss is a marathon, not a sprint. You don't need to do all 10 of these things perfectly from day one. Pick two or three that feel manageable, build consistency, then add more over time. Small, sustainable changes always beat dramatic, short-lived overhauls.
