## Why Cabbage Works So Well for Lower-Calorie Cooking

Cabbage is 92% water, which means it adds serious volume to meals without adding many calories. One pound of raw cabbage costs under $1 at most US grocery stores and yields 4–6 servings. It's also high in vitamin C, vitamin K, and gut-friendly fiber.

## Recipe 1: Cabbage Soup (Classic, ~80 calories per bowl)

This is the foundational cabbage recipe — filling, warming, and extremely low in calories.

**Ingredients (serves 6):**
- ½ head green cabbage, chopped (~4 cups)
- 1 can diced tomatoes (14 oz)
- 3 stalks celery, sliced
- 2 carrots, diced
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 4 cups low-sodium vegetable or chicken broth
- 1 tsp Italian seasoning
- Salt and pepper to taste

**Instructions:**
Sauté onion, celery, and carrots in a large pot with cooking spray for 5 minutes. Add garlic and cook 1 minute. Add cabbage, tomatoes, broth, and seasoning. Bring to a boil, reduce heat, and simmer 20–25 minutes until cabbage is tender.

**Per serving: ~80 calories | 4g protein | 16g carbs | 1g fat | 4g fiber**

Add 1 cup cooked white beans to boost protein to ~10g per bowl for only 120 calories.

## Recipe 2: Cabbage Dumplings (Steamed, ~35 calories each)

Cabbage dumplings are the breakout star of the 2026 trend. These use cabbage leaves as the wrapper instead of flour-based dough, cutting calories dramatically.

**Ingredients (makes 12 dumplings):**
- 12 large cabbage leaves, blanched 2 minutes
- 8 oz lean ground turkey or pork
- 1 cup shredded cabbage (for filling)
- 2 green onions, minced
- 1 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
- 1 clove garlic, minced

**Instructions:**
Mix turkey, shredded cabbage, green onions, soy sauce, sesame oil, ginger, and garlic. Place 1 heaping tablespoon of filling in the center of each blanched cabbage leaf. Fold sides in and roll up. Steam in a bamboo steamer or covered skillet with ½ inch of water for 12–15 minutes.

**Per dumpling: ~35 calories | 4g protein | 2g carbs | 1g fat**
**Per serving of 4 dumplings: ~140 calories | 16g protein**

Serve with a dipping sauce of 1 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tsp rice vinegar + ½ tsp chili flakes (~10 calories).

## Recipe 3: Cabbage Alfredo (~280 calories per serving)

This is the most viral cabbage recipe of 2026. Shredded cabbage replaces pasta, and a lightened-up cream sauce makes it feel indulgent at a fraction of the calories of traditional fettuccine alfredo (~600 calories).

**Ingredients (serves 4):**
- 1 small head green cabbage, thinly shredded (~6 cups)
- 1 tbsp butter
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- ½ cup low-fat cream cheese (4 oz)
- ½ cup low-sodium chicken broth
- ¼ cup grated Parmesan
- Salt, pepper, and nutmeg to taste
- Optional: 4 oz grilled chicken breast per serving (+35g protein)

**Instructions:**
Sauté shredded cabbage in a large skillet over medium-high heat with cooking spray for 8–10 minutes until softened and lightly golden. Remove and set aside. In the same pan, melt butter, add garlic, and cook 1 minute. Add cream cheese and broth, whisking until smooth. Stir in Parmesan. Return cabbage to pan and toss to coat.

**Per serving (without chicken): ~280 calories | 10g protein | 18g carbs | 18g fat**
**With 4 oz grilled chicken: ~450 calories | 45g protein**

## Recipe 4: Stir-Fried Cabbage with Garlic (~90 calories per serving)

The fastest cabbage recipe on this list — ready in 10 minutes and works as a side dish for any protein.

**Ingredients (serves 4):**
- ½ head green or napa cabbage, chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp rice vinegar
- Pinch of red pepper flakes

**Instructions:**
Heat a wok or large skillet over high heat. Add cooking spray, then garlic. Stir-fry 30 seconds. Add cabbage and toss constantly for 4–5 minutes until wilted but still slightly crisp. Add soy sauce, sesame oil, and vinegar. Toss and serve.

**Per serving: ~90 calories | 3g protein | 12g carbs | 3g fat**

## Recipe 5: Cabbage and Ground Turkey Bowl (~350 calories per serving)

A complete meal in one pan. High protein, high fiber, and ready in 20 minutes.

**Ingredients (serves 4):**
- 1 lb lean ground turkey (93% lean)
- 4 cups shredded cabbage
- 1 cup shredded carrots
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
- 1 tbsp hoisin sauce
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 2 green onions, sliced

**Instructions:**
Brown ground turkey in a large skillet over medium-high heat, breaking it up as it cooks. Add garlic and cook 1 minute. Add cabbage and carrots, stir-fry 5 minutes. Add soy sauce, hoisin, and sesame oil. Toss well and top with green onions.

**Per serving: ~350 calories | 32g protein | 18g carbs | 14g fat**

## Recipe 6: Roasted Cabbage Steaks (~120 calories per serving)

Roasting transforms cabbage into something caramelized and almost sweet. Great as a side or a base for toppings.

**Ingredients (serves 4):**
- 1 head green cabbage, cut into 1-inch rounds
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- Salt and pepper

**Instructions:**
Preheat oven to 425°F. Brush cabbage rounds with olive oil on both sides. Season with garlic powder, paprika, salt, and pepper. Roast on a baking sheet for 20 minutes, flip, and roast another 15 minutes until edges are golden and crispy.

**Per serving: ~120 calories | 3g protein | 14g carbs | 7g fat**

Top with a dollop of Greek yogurt and fresh herbs for extra protein.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is cabbage good for weight loss?**
Cabbage is one of the most calorie-efficient vegetables available — 22 calories per cup raw. Its high water and fiber content helps you feel full. It works well as a volume food in any calorie-controlled eating plan.

**Q: How many calories are in cabbage soup?**
A standard homemade cabbage soup runs 70–100 calories per bowl depending on ingredients. Adding beans or lean protein increases calories but also significantly boosts satiety and protein content.

**Q: Can I eat cabbage every day?**
Yes, for most people. Cabbage is nutritious and safe to eat daily. If you have thyroid issues, talk to your doctor about raw cruciferous vegetable intake, as large amounts may affect iodine absorption.

**Q: What's the difference between green and napa cabbage?**
Green cabbage is firmer and slightly more bitter — great for soups, roasting, and stir-fries. Napa cabbage is softer and milder — ideal for dumplings and quick stir-fries. Both have similar calorie counts.

**Q: How do I store leftover cabbage?**
Wrap cut cabbage tightly in plastic wrap and refrigerate for up to 5 days. Cooked cabbage dishes keep well in airtight containers for 3–4 days.
