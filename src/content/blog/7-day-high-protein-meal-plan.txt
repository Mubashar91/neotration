A 7-day high protein meal plan takes the guesswork out of eating well. Instead of deciding what to eat three times a day, you follow a structured plan that keeps protein high, calories in a reasonable range, and meals varied enough to stay interesting. This plan targets 1,500–1,800 calories per day with 120–150g of protein — a range that supports fat loss while preserving muscle for most active adults.

Before starting, use the [FitJourney USA Calorie Calculator](/calculator) to confirm your personal daily calorie target. The plan below works well for most people but can be scaled up or down by adjusting portion sizes.

## How This Plan Is Structured

- Calories: 1,500–1,800 per day
- Protein: 120–150g per day
- Meals: 3 main meals + 1 snack
- Prep time: Most meals take 20–30 minutes; batch cooking on Sunday cuts weekday time to under 10 minutes

## Meal Prep Tips for This Plan

Cook all proteins on Sunday: grill chicken breasts, bake salmon, brown ground turkey. Cook a large batch of quinoa and brown rice. Hard-boil 6 eggs. Store everything in labeled containers. Weekday meals then take under 10 minutes to assemble.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How much protein do I need per day?**
Most active adults benefit from 0.7–1g of protein per pound of body weight. For a 150 lb person, that's 105–150g per day. This plan delivers 120–133g, which fits most people in that range.

**Q: Can I swap meals between days?**
Yes. All meals in this plan are interchangeable. If you prefer Tuesday's breakfast over Monday's, swap them. The key is keeping total daily protein above 100g and calories within your target range.

**Q: Is this plan suitable for vegetarians?**
The plan as written is not vegetarian, but you can substitute animal proteins with lentils, chickpeas, tofu, tempeh, edamame, and cottage cheese. Aim for the same protein totals per meal.

**Q: What if I'm hungrier than the plan allows?**
Add more non-starchy vegetables (broccoli, spinach, cucumber, zucchini) — they're very low in calories and high in volume. You can also add a second snack of Greek yogurt or cottage cheese.

**Q: Do I need to count calories exactly?**
Not necessarily. Use the plan as a template and adjust portions based on hunger and energy. The goal is consistency over perfection — hitting your protein target most days matters more than exact calorie counts.
