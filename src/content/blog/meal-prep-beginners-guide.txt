## Why Meal Prep Works

The biggest barrier to eating healthy isn't willpower — it's convenience. When you're tired after work and there's nothing ready to eat, the path of least resistance is fast food or delivery. Meal prep eliminates that problem entirely.

Research consistently shows that people who prepare their own meals at home consume fewer calories, eat more nutrients, and spend significantly less money on food than those who eat out frequently.

## What You Need to Get Started

You don't need fancy equipment. The basics are: a large baking sheet, a skillet, a pot for grains, food storage containers (glass or BPA-free plastic), and a sharp knife. Invest in a set of matching containers with lids — it makes storing and stacking in the fridge much easier.

## The 4-Category Meal Prep System

The most efficient way to meal prep is to cook in categories: protein, carbs, vegetables, and sauces. Mix and match throughout the week for variety without cooking multiple complete meals.

Proteins (pick 2): Baked chicken breast, ground beef or turkey, hard-boiled eggs, baked salmon, canned tuna

Carbs (pick 2): Brown rice, quinoa, sweet potatoes, oats, whole wheat pasta

Vegetables (pick 3): Roasted broccoli, sautéed spinach, roasted bell peppers, steamed green beans, sliced cucumbers

Sauces (pick 1–2): Salsa, hummus, Greek yogurt dressing, hot sauce, balsamic vinaigrette

## Step-by-Step Sunday Prep (2 Hours)

0:00 — Preheat oven to 400°F. Season chicken breasts and place on a baking sheet. Chop vegetables for roasting and place on a second sheet. Put rice or quinoa on the stove.

0:15 — Chicken and veggies go in the oven. While they cook, boil eggs in a pot and brown ground beef in a skillet. Prep overnight oats for 5 mornings in mason jars.

0:45 — Pull chicken and veggies from oven. Slice chicken, portion rice, divide everything into containers.

1:15 — Cool everything down before sealing containers and placing in the fridge or freezer.

1:45 — Label containers with the day and contents. Prep any snacks (cut fruit, portion nuts, fill yogurt cups).

2:00 — Done. Your entire week of food is ready.

## Sample Week of Meals From One Prep Session

- Breakfast: Overnight oats with banana and almond butter (all 5 days)
- Lunch: Chicken and rice bowl with roasted broccoli and salsa
- Dinner: Ground beef stir-fry with quinoa and bell peppers
- Snacks: Hard-boiled eggs, Greek yogurt, mixed nuts, fruit

## How to Calculate Calories for Your Meal Preps

Portion sizes matter for weight management. Use the FitJourney USA calorie calculator to find your daily calorie goal, then divide it across your meals to set portion targets. For example, on a 1,800-calorie plan you might aim for 400 calories at breakfast, 500 at lunch, 600 at dinner, and 300 across two snacks.

Weigh your proteins and grains with a kitchen scale for the most accurate calorie tracking.

## Meal Prep Tips for Long-Term Success

Start small — prep just lunches for the first week, then expand to dinners.

Cook proteins plain and add sauces later to prevent sogginess.

Most prepped food stays fresh 4–5 days in the fridge. Freeze anything for days 4–5 if you're unsure.

Rotate your recipes every 2–3 weeks to avoid boredom.

## Final Thoughts

Meal prepping is one of the highest-return habits you can build for your health. Two hours on Sunday can save you 5–7 hours of cooking during the week, hundreds of dollars in takeout, and thousands of calories in unhealthy food choices.
